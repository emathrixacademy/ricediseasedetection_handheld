How it works

  1. The needle enters the leaf; the hydrogel takes up sap.
  2. The DAC, through the control amplifier, steps the working electrode's potential (H2O2: −0.2 V to +0.6 V in 4 mV steps; nitrate: 0 to −0.8 V, settings provisional).
  3. Each step adds a 50 mV pulse for 50 ms; the current before and at the end of the pulse is read, and the difference is the DPV signal.
  4. H2O2 is oxidised near +0.25 V (a positive peak); nitrate is reduced near −0.45 V (a negative peak).
  5. The ESP32 marks the reading invalid if the current goes beyond ±16.5 µA (saturated) or the curve is flat (electrode not in contact), then sends the full curve to the Pi 5, which measures the peak height, position, area, width, background slope, number of peaks and signal-to-noise.
